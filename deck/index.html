03 · Aetiology drives duration

Seek the driver, not just the clot

MVT is often the meeting point of a local abdominal trigger and a systemic prothrombotic tendency.

Describe CT as both a diagnosis and a staging investigation. Do not use duplex in place of CT to map acute MVT and bowel consequences.

05 · The decision that changes everything

MEDICAL-FIRST PATH

Start anticoagulation and observe the trajectory.

Repeated examination is the safety system. Worsening pain, sepsis or new peritonism changes the branch.

UFH / LMWH → serial assessment → investigate driver
The clot burden alone does not decide surgery. Bowel viability does.
Clinical deterioration overrides a reassuring-looking early snapshot
This is the vital branching point. Peritonitis, perforation, organ failure, or suspected non-viable bowel requires urgent surgical assessment.

06 · Acute management

First move: stop propagation

FIRST-LINE

UFH or
LMWH

Start therapeutic anticoagulation promptly unless there is a major contraindication.

01Prevent extension

Reduce propagation and recurrent thrombosis.

02Allow recanalisation

Restore drainage where possible.

03Protect bowel

Buy time before venous injury becomes irreversible.

04Support physiology

Fluids, analgesia, electrolyte correction, bowel rest.

Why UFH may be preferred when the abdomen is unstable:it can be rapidly adjusted and reversed if laparotomy becomes necessary. The choice remains contextual.
ESVS 2025 · Recommendation 50 · UFH or LMWH first-line
Supportive treatment matters but is not an alternative to anticoagulation. Antibiotics are situation-specific, not automatically required for uncomplicated MVT.

07 · Rescue, not routine

Escalate for deterioration, not for drama

OBSERVE

Improving

Continue anticoagulation, support, and causal work-up.

→
RESCUE

Worsening on treatment

Selected centres may consider catheter-directed thrombolysis and/or thrombectomy.

→
SURGERY

Peritonitis / necrosis

Urgent operative assessment; preserve maximum viable bowel length.

BOUNDARY

Endovascular venous intervention is an IIb, low-certainty rescue option for clinical deterioration during anticoagulation. It does not displace early heparin or surgery for a surgical abdomen.

ESVS 2025 · Recommendation 51 · rescue escalation in deteriorating patients
Avoid overselling endovascular therapy. Evidence is mainly observational and bleeding risk needs careful weighting.
