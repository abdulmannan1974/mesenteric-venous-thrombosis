Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
AMVT is the least common cause of acute mesenteric ischaemia, 11.5 per cent, about one in a thousand emergency admissions. In-hospital mortality is now around 10 per cent, against roughly 50 per cent for arterial occlusion and about 70 per cent for non-occlusive ischaemia. Incidence estimates vary five-fold between Sweden (2.7 per 100,000, with an 87 per cent autopsy rate) and Finland (0.5 per 100,000): that is ascertainment, not biology, so it is under-diagnosed. Sources: Tamme 2022, AMESI 2024, Acosta and Salim 2021, as cited in ESVS 2025 section 7.2.

02 · Say it precisely

Four terms that are not interchangeable

MVT

Mesenteric venous thrombosis

Thrombus in the superior mesenteric vein, with or without extension into the portal or splenic vein.

AMVT

Acute

Symptom onset within four weeks of presentation. The cut-off is explicitly arbitrary.

CMVT

Chronic

Symptoms beyond four weeks without bowel infarction, or MVT found incidentally on imaging.

VMI

Venous mesenteric ischaemia

Acute symptoms in the presence of AMVT. This, not the clot on the scan, is the guideline's focus.

Outside Chapter 7: Budd–Chiari · isolated portal vein obstruction · sinusoidal obstruction syndrome · hepatic disease from heart failure or chemotherapy.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
A standard viva opener. Acute and chronic are successive stages of one disease with the same causes, but they are managed differently. The exclusions matter because much of the anticoagulation evidence comes from splanchnic vein thrombosis cohorts that include isolated portal and splenic vein thrombosis, and outcomes differ by segment. When someone quotes a trial, ask which veins it studied.

03 · Mechanism

A blocked drain, not a blocked tap

Arterial — cross-linked
✓Perfusion buffered: the block is bypassed.
Venous — no cross-links
!Blood keeps arriving and cannot leave.
Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Both panels block the middle vessel in the same place. On the arterial side the pancreaticoduodenal and marginal arcades carry flow round the block. On the venous side there is no equivalent cross-link, so blood keeps arriving and cannot leave. That is why MVT is congestion before it is ischaemia, and why the early abdomen can be deceptively soft. Ask the room to predict: if venous pressure keeps rising, what happens to capillary inflow? Portosystemic collaterals do form in chronic MVT (portal cavernoma), but they take weeks and do not help in the acute event. Arcade anatomy: ESVS 2025 section 2.2.

04

Haemorrhagic infarction

Fever and peritoneal signs announce it.

Untreated SMV thrombosis carries a 33–45% risk of haemorrhagic bowel infarction. That is why we anticoagulate before we can see necrosis.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
The anatomical point from the guideline: bowel infarction mostly requires involvement of the venous arcades and vasa recta, which causes complete venous occlusion. Until then the segment is usually salvageable, which is the rationale for anticoagulating and observing. The size and extent of the thrombus largely drive outcome. Mucosal injury is not the same as full-thickness infarction: the submucosal network can divert flow away from the mucosa while preserving muscularis and serosa (ESVS section 2.2). ESVS section 7.3.3 gives the 33 to 45 per cent figure.

Name two variants: pylephlebitis — septic portal thrombophlebitis after appendicitis, pancreatitis or diverticulitis · portal cavernoma — chronic collaterals and portal hypertension.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Fever and peritoneal signs are not the presentation of MVT; they are the presentation of its complication. Acute presentation accounts for 20 to 74 per cent of cases depending on the segment, and is more common when the mesenteric veins are involved. Pylephlebitis: high fever, malaise, tenderness and sepsis, sometimes with liver abscesses; antibiotics are indicated. Portal cavernoma: complete portal vein occlusion is virtually always associated with portal hypertension; variceal bleeding and portal cholangiopathy are the classical but rare presentations. ESVS section 7.3.3.

05 · Diagnosis

One test settles it

Recommendation 49 · Class I · Level C

Contrast CT, arterial + portal venous phases

  • ≤ 1 mm slices, optional non-contrast run
  • Urgent, regardless of renal function (Rec 33)
  • Write “?mesenteric ischaemia” on the request (Rec 32)

Vascular findings

Thrombus in the SMV, often extending into the portal vein; then splenic and inferior mesenteric veins.

Bowel findings

Wall thickening · dilatation · mesenteric fat stranding → pneumatosis and portal venous gas when advanced.

Duplex alone is not enough: it cannot show the extent of thrombosis or the bowel changes. CT decides.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
This answers the question from slide 2. The venous phase shows both the extent of thrombosis and the secondary bowel findings; extent is prognostic, since complete recanalisation is associated with less extensive thrombosis. Recommendation 49 was rephrased in 2025 and its level of evidence downgraded from B to C, but it remains Class I. Recommendation 32 is new: stating the suspicion on the request is associated with better diagnosis and outcomes. Diagnostic laparoscopy or laparotomy may be needed in some patients, especially with peritonitis, but distinguishing reversible from irreversible ischaemia is notably difficult in AMVT.

06 · Acute management

Heparin first, in everyone

Recommendation 50 · Class I · Level C

UFH or LMWH

  • Start soon after diagnosis unless there is a major contraindication
  • UFH is reversible with protamine if laparotomy or bleeding follows
  • Early anticoagulation recanalises 40–80%

Supportive care, at once

Pain control · fluids and electrolytes · bowel rest. NG aspiration for ileus; transfusion if bleeding; parenteral nutrition.

Antibiotics are not routine

Give for perforation, translocation sepsis, pylephlebitis or septic thrombophlebitis.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Most patients with AMVT are treated successfully with therapeutic anticoagulation and supportive treatment alone. The goal is to stop propagation and promote recanalisation, preventing infarction, portal hypertension and recurrence. Antibiotics: two small retrospective studies showed no mortality or length-of-stay benefit in MVT. Note the contrast with arterial AMI, where Recommendation 41 is Class I for antibiotics; ESVS says the Chapter 5 bowel-management recommendations also apply to AMVT, so be ready to explain the nuance. ESVS sections 7.5.1 and 7.5.2.

07 · Escalation

Escalate for deterioration, not clot size

Redrawn from ESVS 2025 Figure 6 (after Acosta & Salim 2021).

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Walk the arrows. Everything that develops peritonitis goes up to surgery. Endovascular therapy sits in one place only: a patient worsening on anticoagulation without peritonitis. Options include TIPS with aspiration thrombectomy, direct venous thrombolysis with or without angioplasty, percutaneous transhepatic thrombectomy or thrombolysis, and thrombolysis via the SMA; the direct venous route is used in more centres and may be more effective. Surgery aims to remove irreversibly ischaemic bowel and preserve length; the resection rate in a systematic review of 599 patients was 43.9 per cent.

Our patient is worsening on anticoagulation but has no peritonitis, which is exactly where Recommendation 51 applies. Rec 46 · Class I · Level B: in every patient, look for intra-abdominal malignancy, inflammatory disease, MPN, CMV and SARS-CoV-2 infection, and chronic liver disease.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
The trap: a local trigger does not exclude a systemic cause. Appendicitis or pancreatitis can provoke MVT, but systemic factors are present in 60 to 70 per cent overall, so the sets overlap. Pooled prevalences from 1725 patients: cirrhosis or portal hypertension 28.8 per cent, liver disease 27.8, pancreatitis 11.1, inflammatory bowel disease 10.0 (Wu 2023). Recommendation 46 is one of only two Level B recommendations in the chapter. ESVS section 7.3.1.

Thrombophilia screen

Antiphospholipid syndrome

Recurrent MVT and recurrent foetal loss. Rec 47 · IIa

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
The haematologist's slide. JAK2 V617F is a diagnostic test for a clonal disease, not part of a thrombophilia screen. In 52 per cent of mutation-positive patients the MPN declared itself only on follow-up, with MVT as its first manifestation. Treat the odds ratio as directional: the interval runs from 13 to 222. On thrombophilia testing, ESVS mirrors ASH 2023: test only where the result could change the plan; with another indication for anticoagulation there is no indication to test. Remember assay timing: lupus anticoagulant is unreliable on anticoagulation, antithrombin falls on heparin, protein C and S fall on warfarin. ESVS sections 7.3.1 and 7.3.2.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Recommendations 52 to 56. The middle column is the one to dwell on: in most VTE a resolved transient factor is the reason to stop at three months, but ESVS 2025 says extension beyond six months should be considered here, because recurrence in this bed can cost bowel. Combined with Recommendation 56, almost every patient with MVT is a candidate for anticoagulation beyond six months. Recommendation 52 is one of only two Level B recommendations; Recommendation 56 is graded on consensus. Rivaroxaban and apixaban use is off-label, supported by small retrospective data.

Dr Abdul Mannan FRCPath FCPS · Blood🩸Doctor · contact@blooddoctor.co
Both large datasets show more thrombosis after stopping. On bleeding they point in opposite directions: higher after stopping in the IPD meta-analysis (confounding by indication; sicker patients, especially with cirrhosis and varices, both stop and bleed) and lower after stopping in the registry. That disagreement is the honest summary of the evidence, and why Recommendation 56 rests on consensus. Note that both are splanchnic vein cohorts, broader than MVT; in the IPD dataset only 1.7 per cent used a DOAC. Pooled meta-analysis of anticoagulation against stopping: recanalisation RR 2.39, major bleeding RR 0.73, mortality RR 0.45, recurrence RR 0.91 (no difference). ESVS section 7.6. Segment matters too: ten-year recurrence-free survival was 97 per cent for isolated splenic vein thrombosis against 60 per cent for isolated MVT (Thatipelli 2010).
